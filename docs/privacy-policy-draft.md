## 2. What data we collect, and why

This section is built directly from the real data map produced by this project's own technical audit (2026-09-18), not from a template.

| Data | What it is | Why we collect it | Where it's actually processed/stored |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phone number | Your mobile number | To create and secure your account (OTP login) | Never stored as plain text — only a keyed cryptographic hash is kept, so we ourselves cannot read your raw phone number back out of our database (`User.phoneHash`) |
| Liveness check | A live selfie taken during signup | To confirm a real person, not a bot or a stolen photo, created the account | **The image itself is never stored anywhere, by design** — only a pass/fail result and a timestamp are kept. No image, video frame, or biometric template of any kind is retained (see §6 below for an important limitation of what this actually proves) |
| Profile information | Name, birth date, city, bio, interests, and similar fields you fill in | To show your profile to other users and power discovery/matching | Stored directly on your account record |
| Photos | Profile pictures you upload | To show your profile to other users | Stored in our object storage after being scanned for malware and reviewed for policy violations; EXIF metadata (which can contain location data from your camera) is stripped automatically |
| Messages | Text you send to a match | To deliver your conversation | Stored so your conversation history is available when you open the app again; visible only to you and the person you matched with |
| Reports & blocks | Information you submit when reporting or blocking another user | To review safety reports and keep you from seeing someone you've blocked | Visible to our safety/moderation team; the person you report is not told who reported them |
| Bio "fingerprint" (embedding) | A mathematical representation of your bio text, generated by an AI model | To power semantic search and matching ("find people who mention similar things") | Stored as a numeric vector attached to your profile; not human-readable and not shown to anyone |
| Call metadata | Technical details about a voice/video call (e.g., connection quality) — **never the audio/video content itself** | To detect and report connection problems | Stored on the call record; no recording of any call is ever made |
| Search/AI usage | The fact that you used AI-powered search or the guided profile-setup assistant, when, and basic cost/performance metadata | To operate and improve these features and manage costs | The content of what you typed or said in these features is **not stored** after the conversation ends — only the fact that you used the feature is kept |

## 3. Legal basis for processing

**[NEEDS LEGAL INPUT — this entire section requires counsel.]** Likely candidates per data category, for counsel to confirm or replace:
- Phone number, profile info, photos, messages: performance of the contract you enter into by creating an account (providing the dating service you signed up for).
- Liveness/biometric check: **explicit consent** (see §4) — biometric data is a "special category" under GDPR Article 9 and needs its own, separately documented lawful basis; contract-performance is unlikely to be sufficient on its own for this category. This must not be assumed without a completed DPIA (`docs/legal-checklist.md` Part B).
- Safety reports: legitimate interest (protecting users) and/or legal obligation, depending on jurisdiction.

## 4. Biometric consent

The app requires an explicit, separate consent step before the liveness check runs. The real, current consent text shown to users (Persian, translated here for review):

> "To build trust between users, we use a live selfie to make sure a real person is behind this profile. This photo is used only for this moment and is deleted immediately — no ID document is stored. By continuing, you declare your consent to this biometric verification."

**[NEEDS LEGAL INPUT — a real, material gap found by this project's own audit, flagged here so it isn't missed]:** this consent text states the selfie proves "a real person is behind this profile," but the system does **not** actually verify that your *current* profile photo is the same person who passed the liveness check — there is no face-matching of any kind. The "verified" badge shown to other users only ever means "this account passed a one-time liveness check at some point," not "the photos you're looking at are this same verified person." **Counsel should determine whether the current consent wording overstates what liveness verification actually accomplishes**, and whether this needs to be corrected in the consent flow, the privacy policy, or both, before this is disclosed to real users at scale.

## 5. Who your information is shared with

- **Other users**: your profile, photos, and (if you've completed a match) messages are visible to people you match with, per the app's normal functioning. A "Share My Date" feature (if you use it) shares a match's name, photo, and verified status with a third party you choose, who has no account with us — **[NEEDS LEGAL INPUT: this specific disclosure to a non-user third party needs its own explicit policy language, per `docs/legal-checklist.md` Part D — a generic "we share with other users" clause is not sufficient for this feature]**.
- **Service providers who process data on our behalf** (all currently used in this app, real vendors, not hypothetical):
  - **OpenAI** (USA) — processes your bio text, search queries, and photo content transiently to power AI search, match explanations, content moderation, and the guided profile-setup assistant. **[NEEDS LEGAL INPUT: a Data Processing Agreement with OpenAI, and a valid cross-border transfer mechanism (since this is a US company), are not yet in place — see `docs/legal-checklist.md` Part B/E.]**
  - **Neon** (database hosting) — stores your account data.
  - **Upstash** (Redis hosting) — temporarily holds real-time session/presence data.
  - **Object storage provider** (photo/video storage — MinIO in development; a real cloud provider such as Cloudflare R2 is planned for production, not yet finalized).
  - **Cloudflare** — provides the connection-relay service (TURN) that helps voice/video calls connect.
  - **[NEEDS LEGAL INPUT: no Data Processing Agreement currently exists with any of these vendors — this is a real, confirmed gap, not an oversight in this draft.]**
- We do not sell your personal information to third parties for their own marketing purposes.

## 6. A real, current technical limitation you should know about

Because this is meant to be an accurate policy, not a marketing document, one specific limitation deserves plain disclosure once legal wording is settled: passing our liveness check proves you were a live person in front of the camera at signup. It does **not** verify your government ID, and it does **not** re-check that photos you upload later are actually of you. **[NEEDS LEGAL INPUT: how much of this technical reality should be disclosed directly to users, versus handled purely as an internal risk to mitigate — this is a genuine product-trust and consumer-protection question, not only a drafting question.]**

## 7. How long we keep your data

**[NEEDS LEGAL INPUT — most specific retention periods are not yet decided.]** What's confirmed today:
- Your liveness selfie: never stored at all (see §2).
- Everything else tied to your account: kept until you delete your account.
- When you delete your account: your profile, photos (the actual image files, not just the database record), messages, matches, and most other account data are permanently and immediately deleted. A small number of records are deliberately kept even after deletion for legitimate reasons: safety/moderation records where you were involved as an admin reviewer (with your identifying link removed), and internal cost/operational logs for AI usage (again with your identifying link removed). Nothing that identifies you personally is retained in these residual records once your account is deleted.
